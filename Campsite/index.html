The only park on earth bordered by a capital city

Wake up to lions on one side, skyscrapers on the other.

Savanna Edge Camp sits on the southern boundary fence of Nairobi National Park, on a bend of the Mbagathi River. From the same camp chair you can watch giraffe move through long grass in the foreground and the Nairobi skyline catch the evening light behind them — a view that exists nowhere else on the continent.

What's Here

Camping

Pitch your own tent or stay in one of our furnished walk-in tents along the riverbank, inside the electric perimeter fence.

Wildlife Viewing

A raised viewing deck looks straight into the park — no game drive required to spot zebra, giraffe, and the occasional lion.

Guided Nature Walks

Armed rangers lead walks along the buffer zone outside the fence, reading tracks and spotting birds at ground level.

Guided Fishing

Catch-and-release fishing for tilapia and barbel on our stretch of the Mbagathi River and the camp's small dam.

A camp built around one fence line

Nairobi National Park is fenced on three sides but open to the Athi-Kapiti plains on the fourth, which is why wildlife still moves freely along our boundary. We built the camp along that fence deliberately — every tent faces the park, not the car park. On a clear evening you can watch a herd of buffalo graze with the city skyline lit up behind them.

Is this camp inside Nairobi National Park?

No — we sit just outside the park's southern boundary, on private land along the Mbagathi River. Wildlife regularly moves along this stretch, but our nature walks and fishing take place outside the park fence, which is why they don't require a park vehicle or park entry fee.

Wildlife sightings are never guaranteed — this is a wild, unfenced boundary, not a zoo. Rates and availability current as of August 2026; see Stay & Rates.
